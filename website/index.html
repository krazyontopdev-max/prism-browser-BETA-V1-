PRIVACY, IN YOUR HANDS

Choose what gets through.

Prism Shields blocks known tracker and advertising domains. Standard mode targets listed third-party domains; Strict blocks more and can affect websites. If a site needs it, set an exception or switch Shields off.

Prism has no telemetry, analytics, remote diagnostics or crash uploads. Profiles, history, bookmarks and settings stay on your device. Websites still receive your requests and IP address, and searches go to your chosen provider.

Clear browsing data, review site permissions, and use private windows for a separate session. Shields cannot block every ad or all tracking.

DESIGNED FOR RESPONSIVENESS

More room for
what matters.

Stay in control of background tabs and the resources they use.

Choose Balanced, Maximum Savings, or Off. Prism can put eligible inactive tabs to sleep to reduce background resource use. Keep Awake protects a tab you want running.

In one synthetic ten-tab test on the development PC, Maximum Savings reduced Prism’s total process-tree private memory from about 786 MiB to 295 MiB. Your results will depend on the sites you use.

Active tabs and important activity receive protections; some background activity cannot be detected reliably. Prism has not yet been fairly benchmarked against Chrome or tested across low-, mid- and high-end PCs.

GOOD TO KNOW

Questions,
answered.

Which platforms does Prism support?

The current build targets Windows x64. Other platforms are not yet available.

How does tab sleeping work?

Prism can freeze or discard eligible inactive tabs. Balanced is the default; Maximum Savings is more aggressive, and Off disables automatic sleeping. Keep Awake protects a chosen tab.

Can I use my own wallpaper?

Yes. You can choose a local image, rotate custom images, mix them with bundled images, select categories, or use no wallpaper.

Does Prism sync my data?

Prism stores its browser data locally. Cloud synchronization is not listed among its current features.

Will every website and video work?

Compatibility varies. The codec-enabled build passed local media tests, but streaming services, authenticated calls, DRM and some security checks need further acceptance.
